5 Questions First-Time VA Buyers Ask Me Most

Straightforward answers to the credit score, funding fee, eligibility, and timeline questions I hear most from buyers around Camden County and Kings Bay

Buying your first home can feel overwhelming, especially when you're also trying to understand how your VA benefits fit into the process. The good news is that most of the questions I hear have straightforward answers.

Questions about credit scores, funding fees, eligibility, and the timeline are some of the most common topics I hear from buyers around Camden County and Kings Bay. The good news is that the VA loan process is usually simpler than people expect. Once you understand the steps, it becomes much easier to move forward with confidence.

Question #1: Am I Actually Eligible for a VA Loan?

The VA loan program was created to help military members and veterans become homeowners. Instead of requiring large down payments like conventional loans, the VA backs a portion of the loan for lenders. This allows qualified buyers to purchase a home with more flexible requirements.

Before starting the home search, it's helpful to understand the main eligibility factors lenders look at when reviewing a VA loan application.

  • Credit Score: While the VA itself does not set a minimum credit score, most lenders look for scores around the low-to-mid 600s. Buyers with stronger credit typically receive more favorable interest rates.
  • Certificate of Eligibility (COE): This document confirms your VA loan entitlement. I can obtain it electronically during the pre-qualification process.
  • Primary Residence Requirement: VA loans are designed for homes you plan to live in, not investment properties or vacation homes.

If you're unsure about your eligibility or entitlement, reviewing a full guide like the Free VA Loan Guide can help clarify the basics before starting the process.

Question #2: What Is the VA Funding Fee, and Do I Have to Pay It?

One of the most common questions from first-time VA buyers involves the funding fee. This fee helps keep the VA loan program running and reduces the cost to taxpayers while allowing the program to offer zero-down financing.

The funding fee is typically calculated as a percentage of the loan amount and varies depending on factors like your down payment and whether you've used your VA loan benefit before.

Important Things to Know About the Funding Fee

Many buyers are relieved to learn that the funding fee does not usually need to be paid upfront. In most cases, it can be rolled into the loan amount and financed over time. Some veterans with qualifying service-connected disabilities may also be exempt from paying the fee entirely.

The exact percentage can change periodically, so it's always best to review current guidelines when planning a purchase.

Question #3: What Credit Score Do I Need to Qualify?

After learning about eligibility and the VA funding fee, the next question many buyers ask is whether their credit score is high enough to qualify.

The good news is that the Department of Veterans Affairs does not set a minimum credit score requirement for VA loans. Instead, individual lenders establish their own lending guidelines based on the overall risk of the loan.

Many lenders look for credit scores in the low-to-mid 600s, but your credit score is only one piece of the puzzle.

When reviewing your application, lenders also consider factors such as:

  • Your income and employment history
  • Your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio
  • Available assets and reserves, when applicable
  • Your recent credit history and overall financial profile

A lower credit score doesn't automatically mean you won't qualify. In many cases, there are steps you can take to strengthen your application or improve your credit profile before purchasing a home. I can help with that — I have tools and expertise to guide you through the credit improvement process.

That's one reason I encourage buyers to get pre-qualified early. If there are any issues that need attention, it's much easier to address them before you're under contract than after you've found the home you want.

Even if you're unsure whether you're ready, having a conversation with a lender can help you understand where you stand and what your next steps might be.

Question #4: What Happens After I Get Pre-Approved?

Once you're pre-approved, the fun part begins: shopping for your new home. While every transaction is unique, most VA home purchases follow the same general path from offer to closing.

After reviewing your income, credit, and VA eligibility, you'll know how much home you can comfortably afford. From there, you can begin working with your real estate agent to find the right home and submit an offer.

A Different Approach to Pre-Approval

One thing that sets my team apart is what happens before you find a home.

Many lenders wait until your offer is accepted before requesting the documents needed to process your loan. We use that time differently. While you're searching for a home, we begin gathering and reviewing your documentation so your file is ready for the first round of underwriting as soon as you're under contract.

That preparation can help reduce last-minute surprises and keep your loan moving efficiently toward closing.

Question #5: Can We Close Before I Report for Duty?

For many service members, timing is just as important as finding the right home. Whether you're PCSing to Kings Bay or another duty station, you want to know your family will have a place to call home before you report for duty.

While no lender can promise a specific closing date, preparation goes a long way toward keeping your purchase on schedule.

Once your offer is accepted, several important steps happen behind the scenes. We order the VA appraisal, begin title work, and submit your loan for underwriting. The appraisal confirms the home's value and ensures it meets the VA's minimum property requirements, while underwriting completes the final review of your loan.

After your loan receives final approval, you'll attend closing, sign your documents, and receive the keys to your new home.

Many VA purchase loans close in about 30 days (or less) from the date a purchase contract is accepted, although every transaction is different. Factors such as appraisal scheduling, title work, and how quickly requested documentation is provided can all affect the timeline.

That's why I encourage buyers to start the pre-approval process as early as possible. The more preparation we can complete before you're under contract, the better positioned we'll be to keep your closing on track and help make your move as smooth as possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What credit score do I need for a VA loan?

The VA does not set a strict minimum credit score, but most lenders look for scores around 600 or higher. Buyers with stronger credit may qualify for better interest rates and more flexible loan terms.

Do VA loans require a down payment?

One of the biggest benefits of a VA loan is that qualified buyers can purchase a home with no down payment. This allows many military families to buy sooner without waiting years to save a large down payment.

How long does the VA loan process take?

Most VA purchase loans close within about 30 to 45 days after a contract is accepted. The timeline can vary depending on documentation, appraisal scheduling, and underwriting requirements.

Can first-time buyers use a VA loan?

Yes. In fact, many VA loan users are first-time homebuyers. The program is specifically designed to make homeownership more accessible for military members and veterans.

Is the VA funding fee always required?

Not always. Veterans who receive VA disability compensation and certain surviving spouses may qualify for an exemption from the funding fee.

Are there any special homebuyer programs for service members in Georgia?

Yes. Eligible active-duty service members and veterans may qualify for the Georgia Dream Peach Select program, which offers a subsidized interest rate that is often substantially lower than market rates for qualified borrowers and is not limited to first-time homebuyers. Eligibility requirements apply, and my team regularly helps buyers determine whether the program is a good fit for their home purchase.

Key Takeaways

  • The VA loan program allows eligible military buyers to purchase homes with no down payment.
  • Credit score, eligibility documentation, and income verification are key factors in the approval process.
  • The VA funding fee supports the program and can often be financed into the loan.
  • Most VA home purchases close within 30 to 45 days once a contract is accepted.
  • Starting with pre-qualification helps first-time buyers understand their budget and avoid surprises.
